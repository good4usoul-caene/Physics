### Spacetime Interval
A spactime inteval can be spacelike, timelike, or null.  The general idea is that two events may take place at different places in different times.  The question that determines what type of interval you have is "Could both events happen to the same particle?"  The possible answers are
1. **Yes, but** only if that particle happens to be a photon, who is (emitted, refracted, reflected, etc.) from the first event, and (absorbed, refracted, reflected) at the second event.  If the photon moves between one event to the other without undue interference, then it required the **speed of light** to be present at both events.  The interval between these events is called a "null interval".
2. **Yes, and** the particle aged during the time it "went between" the events.  Actually, technically, from the particle's own perspective, it never moved.  Since the particle considers it's own position to be at the origin, and both events occurred at the origin, the distance $\chi$ between the events is zero, but the particle aged by $\tau$.
3. **No**.  The speed $v_\chi=\frac{x}{t}=\frac{c}{\beta_\chi}$ necessary to get from the earlier event to the later event is *faster* than the speed of light, and some observer with speed $v=c\beta_\chi$ would see the two events as *simultaneous*.  For such an observer, the distance between the events is $\chi$ and the time between the events is $\tau=0$.
