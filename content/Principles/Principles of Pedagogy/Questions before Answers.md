Ideally, when addressing questions of physics, a question arises before an answer is proffered.  However, you cannot always count on it.  Unfortunately the pedagogy of upper level physics is rife with topics where books present an answer as doctrine, even though, the teachers cannot even explain what the question is.  In such cases, it may sometimes be possible to find out the question through reverse engineering the problem.

In the real universe, the scientific method mainly has two branches.  One observes data in the world, becomes curious, asks questions, and offers hypotheses that might explain the observed phenomena.  Then they imagine and possibly build tests called experiments to determine whether things have been properly understood.
