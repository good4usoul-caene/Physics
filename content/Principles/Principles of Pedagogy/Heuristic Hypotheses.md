Communicating is not always easy.  When reading about a complicated topic, it can be helpful to borrow a term from Biblical scholars called "heuristics".  Heuristics is essentially the study of what words mean.

Unfortunately, with the Bible, and with any book where the author has passed away, or is for other reasons, not available for interrogation, one must either form a "heuristic hypothesis" - that is, a conjecture about what the words mean, or must lose faith that the author actually "means" anything at all (See [[Never say Meaningless]])

Without the original author, the best one can do is form an idea of what they *think* the author means, and then re-read, and see if the explanation fits.  This is true, unless the original author thought to provide a ==Problem Set== with at least some ==solutions==.  The beauty of a problem set with solutions is that quite often you can at least determine if you understood what was meant if you can get the same answers.  

If you can't get the same answers, or you don't have a problem set, then the best you can hope for is that the author might be alive, so you can ask "what did you mean" or find someone who understood the author, or the idea they were trying to explain.

In any case, a Heuristic Hypothesis can work much in the same way as a Scientific Hypothesis, except the "experiment" of saying "what do you mean?" can be a lot easier than building a contraption with all the necessary parts to test a scientific hypothesis.
