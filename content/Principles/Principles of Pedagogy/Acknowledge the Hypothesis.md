The scientific method is often described with many parts, but there are really two main endeavors. 
1. Understanding what is going on to the point that you can form new hypotheses.
2. Building experiments that can make the distinction between incompatible hypotheses.

Unfortunately, there are times when people are at the point where they can formulate new hypotheses, but they are met with statements such as "that is a misconception", instead of "that has been tested and proven to be incorrect."  

Of course it is legitimate to keep a list of common misconceptions, but such lists should not be misrepresentations of the ideas.  At a minimum one should keep separate what is actually said vs. [[Heuristic Hypotheses]] about what is actually meant.  

In a field where defenders of the canon are often known to say "it is a misconception to believe that distant coordinates have any [[Never say Meaningless|meaning]]" it is often those who hypothesize that their ideas *actually HAVE meaning* who are able to formulate legitimate hypotheses.
