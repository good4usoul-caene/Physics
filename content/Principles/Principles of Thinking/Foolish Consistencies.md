There's a saying that "A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds," however, I used to despise this quote because I didn't understand what to contrast it to.

 1. Contrast with "Don't re-invent the wheel."
 2. Contrast with "Use the same math for the entire domain"

As a defense of re-inventing the wheel, the quote is helpful.  One should not be criticized for thinking through a problem in a manner of their own choosing.  One should not be criticized for asking well-formed questions that no one else has thought to ask.

If the words are spoken as a defense of an inconsistent mathematics, logic, or premises, though, such consistencies are not foolish.  

Of course, everyone will develop their habits, and some habits are foolish, and some habits are wise.  Those habits that are a waste of time or lead you in a wrong direction, are, of course, foolish.  The habits that lead you to progress in an area you desire progress are wise.
