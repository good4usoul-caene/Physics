"Largest Terms First" is a principle that is used in Taylor and Maclaurin series expansions to quickly come to the correct answer of an infinite sum.  One does not add together a huge number of small terms, for instance, to get a bias, and then continue only adding large quantities after subtracting enough small quantities to cancel them out.

As a rule, when dealing with infinite series, if you are actually provided the freedom to add together quantities in whatever order you want, you can often generate whatever answer you want.  The concept goes by the name of the ==Riemannian Rearrangement Theorem==

In General Relativity, one of the ideas that I disavow is that an infinite homogeneous distribution of matter ought to cause different gravitational fields depending on what choice is made as to the center.  The idea is that if you take some such center, you can arrange the terms of gravitational influence so that billions of particles light-years away to your right are counted *before* a SINGLE particle is counted to your left.  Obviously by arranging terms in this fashion one obtains an infinite sum that not only yields a gravitational field to your right, but it continues to do so as long as that bias is maintained in the infinite sum.

I'm sure I've said enough to convince any rational person that an infinite homogeneous distribution causes NO gravitational field on any particle.  However, the idea seems pervasive in the literature, so I have made a video that tries to fully acknowledge the idea why someone might THINK that one can influence a gravitational field by manipulating the ordering of terms in the sum, and why I think such an idea is unjustified.
