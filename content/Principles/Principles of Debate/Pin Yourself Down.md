In a debate of ideas, one should desire to play defense more than offense.  

# Do you have your ideas, or do your ideas have you?

Be careful not to become so attached to your ideas that they become your identity.  While certain firmly held principles may define who we are, we should be selective about what principles define us, versus what ideas represent our [[Leading Hypothesis|Leading Hypotheses]].  It is important not to feel attacked simply because someone suggests there might be a flaw in something we believe.  There is a big difference between saying "*Here is why I think you may be mistaken*" and an [[Ad Hominen]] argument.

But the defense of an idea is not accomplished by "[[Dodging]]".  Dodging is an excellent defense for someone who is in a physical fight.  Dodging is not being where your opponent expects you to be.  However, in an argument over ideas, dodging means that you do not have any real position to defend. 

A good defense means that 
 - You have an idea to defend
 - You understand the idea well enough to defend it.
 - You are willing to explore various questions applicable to the idea.
