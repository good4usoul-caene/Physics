Any claim that one idea may have rendered another idea meaningless is to be avoided.  
 - Sometimes an idea can be irrelevant to a discussion at hand
 - Sometimes an idea can be poorly defined.
 - Sometimes an idea may have no procedural description

However, none of these ideas need necessarily be called "meaningless".  It may be better to say 
 - I have no idea what you mean.  Can you elaborate?
	 - This may not always be successful.  
	 - **Personal Anecdote:** I was told by someone with absolute conviction that the universe is just one of many "bubble universes".  However, such a hypothesis ought to answer some question about what is physically observed.  This person gave no hint as to such a question.  
	 - **Personal Anecdote:** Another person has told me with equal conviction that the universe is not expanding, but each particle is shrinking.  Again, such a hypothesis ought to answer some question about what is physically observed.  In this case, one can imagine thought experiments such as "how would that effect the redshifts of different stars?"  "Do photon wavelengths stay the same size as our bodies shrink?"  "Why do we not notice this shrinking in any way, shape, or form?"  



Popular literature on relativity may have mention of meaninglessness
  - **Personal Anecdote:** I have heard people say that gravity renders distant simultaneity "meaningless".  However, to me, that's rather like saying "gravity has rendered distant *in front of you*" meaningless, or distant *to your right*, meaningless.  The simultaneity plane might be "irrelevant" to me, (See: [[Image Location vs Simultaneous Location]]) but it does not become meaningless or undefined, simply because someone has defined a [[Jacobians & Coordinate Transformations|Jacobian]].
