The Schwarzschild Solution is generally given as follows.  

$$(cd\tau)^2=\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}\right)(cdt)^2-\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}\right)^{-1}dr^2-(rd\phi)^2$$
 
However, it tends to be tied to a question that isn't well phrased.  





 
 Our description of Schwarzschild coordinates should be compatible with two widely accepted experimental results: the [[Precession of Mercury]] and the [[deflection of starlight]].  

To obtain the starlight deflection our main criteria is to assume that the null interval $d\tau=0$ is obtained for the photon at every step of its path.

For the precession of Mercury we may restrict our path based on the particle's relativistic energy and relativistic angular momentum being conserved at every step of its path.

Such considerations might allow us to bypass the calculation of the [[Christoffel Symbols]], however, it would be preferable to show that the methods are equivalent.
