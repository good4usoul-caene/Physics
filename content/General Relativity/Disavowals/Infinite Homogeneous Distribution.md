
### Gravitational Field
It is my expectation that the gravitational field acting on every particle in an infinite homogeneous distribution ought to be zero.
#### Personal Anecdote:  
I have observed an argument being applied to an infinite homogeneous distribution that the gravitational field acting on any particle was based on the choice of an arbitrary point in the distribution.

The argument was based on the premise that infinite homogeneous distributions have **spherical symmetry**, and that a gravitational pull could be obtained based on picking an arbitrary center.  **Newton's Shell Theorem** was used to justify the claim that every shell beyond the radius from the arbitrarily chosen point would cancel out.

However, an infinite homogeneous distribution also has **cylindrical symmetry** around every arbitrarily chosen central axis, and **planar symmetry** around every arbitrarily chosen central plane.  

Since no choice of point, line, or plane gives the same answer for the gravitational field at any point, the most conservative expectation for the gravitational field on a particle in an infinite homogeneous distribution is that it would be zero.
