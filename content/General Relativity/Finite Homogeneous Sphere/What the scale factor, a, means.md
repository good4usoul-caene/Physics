We have three separate r's to consider.  r(t) can be associated with any particle in the system.  $r_\text{max}(t_1)$ is the radius of the full distribution at time $t_1$.  $r_\text{max}(t)$ is the radius of the full sphere at any time.  

$$\rho_1=\frac{M}{V_1}
\\=\frac{3M}{4\pi r_\text{max}(t_1)^3}
$$

Can I get a simple expression for $\rho(t)$?   How about one for $a$?  We're using $a$ to represent a function of t that expresses the overall scale of the distribution.  For instance

$$a=\frac{r_\text{max}(t)}{r_\text{max}(t_1)}
\\=\frac{r(t)}{r(t_1)}$$
Since $$V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$
We can replace $r(t)=a(t)\cdot r(t_1)$ 

If you're confused, you're not alone.  $r(t_1)$ is a constant; being not the initial position of an arbitrary particle within the sphere, but it's position at some time $t_1$ after an initial time.  $r(t)$ reflects the changing position of this arbitrary particle.  $a(t)$ imposes an assumption that the whole sphere grows or shrinks uniformly.

I would like to consider the idea of renaming $r(t_1)$ as $r_{p,t_1}$ .  It will cancel out of our analysis, but $p$ represents its association with a *particle* and $t_1$ represents it's "initial-like" state.
