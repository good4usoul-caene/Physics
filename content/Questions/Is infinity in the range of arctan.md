As we approach an angle of 90 degrees should we obtain an "undefined" slope  $\pm \infty$?  

But wouldn't it be neat if we had a better tangent function?

If we had a tangent function that alternated back and forth, we could have one that unambiguously approached $+\infty$  at $\pi/2$ radians (90 degrees), whether you are coming from lower or higher.

For all physical applications, this is actually what happens.  When we turn knobs or spin disks, there is no particular ambiguity about turning the knob by 90 degrees.  Geometry does not balk at rendering an infinite slope, and if we were to take the definition of tangent from physical inspiration rather than esoteric mathematical inspirations, this ought to be its true form:

So yes, I would say that infinity is in the range, at least, of this alternative form of tangent, and we should not be shy about saying there is a difference between the inverse being "undefined" and being "infinite."  

The normal tangent function has a gigantic jump from $-\infty$ to $\infty$ which means its inverse function AT 90 degrees and 270 degrees is undefined.  But when we actually picture the angle and which direction the vector is pointed, 90 degrees is definitely pointed UP with infinite slope and 270 degrees is definitely pointed DOWN with infinite slope.
