Dingle's objection is described and answered in the paper here:
 - [(PDF) Is there a solid counter-argument against Dingle's old objection to Relativity Theory?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352998464_Is_there_a_solid_counter-argument_against_Dingle's_old_objection_to_Relativity_Theory)

The primary symmetry of the thought experiment is that we may obtain two synced clocks from one inertial frame, and a single clock passing between them.  This is the only way that the times on relatively moving clocks can be compared.

Some people might argue, "no, there's no clock over there".  However, they will still use the notion that the particle reached a certain point in space at a certain time.  As soon as they acknowledge "at a certain time" they have placed an implicit [[ghost clock]]
