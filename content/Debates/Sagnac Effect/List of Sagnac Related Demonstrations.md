| [Rel-Roll-Wheel](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/670c6e9ed0)                            | A 2D+T animation showing the [[Ehrenfest Phenomenon]], and it's association with the [[Sagnac Effect]]. If it retains it's apparent length, then it must stretch.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| [Rindler Circle](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/ccjhilwfdj)                            | This animation visualizes a set of points, or "train cars", accelerating around a circle in such a way that they remain rigid in their own inertial frame, but they contract in the "railroad track" track frame.  To the right, a similar set of points is shown (except they are arranged in a straight line instead of around a circle), coming in from the right, and accelerating to the right.  The [[Spacetime Diagram]] is shown in green.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| [Rindler Pair in Inertial and Rocket Frame](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/b7f749f74a) | One of the main questions in General Relativity is to ask, when a rocket is accelerating through space, how does the frequency of a light received at the top of the vehicle compare to the frequency of the light emitted at the bottom of the vehicle, and vice versa? | [Sagnac Holonomy](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/bbixckfhzr)                           | When imagining the global Sagnac Effect, we are imagining a signal going forward from the beam splitter and back to the beam splitter, in the forward direction and in the backward direction.  In order to catch up to the beam splitter from behind, the ray must go take a longer amount of time.  In order to hit the beam splitter from the front, the ray takes a lesser amount of time.  <br><br>We could, however, have two or three different clocks at the beam-splitter's location with different times.  By selecting the right clocks, we can "cheat" and make it seem that the forward ray and the backward ray took the same amount of time. |
| [Sagnac Interference Desync](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/7domih5e45)                | If the signal is to have no interference pattern, the forward (red) signal must be released before the backward (green) signal.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| [Center-Synchronization](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/uqsbrtz3mw)                    | For clocks moving at less than the speed of light a distance $r$ from a central axle, it is possible to continuously sync them from a central signal.  They can also be calibrated to keep time with that central signal, though they would be naturally inclined to run slower than the central signal.  (From the rim observer's perspective, the central signal is [[aberrated]] forward and [[blue-shifted]])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
