The "space-time interval" between two events is, mathematically, an invariant quantity, but it is different from coordinate time or the Euclidean Distance, which are affected by the observer's speed.



Here's how the invariant Minkowski metric ought to be compared to time and Euclidean distance:

  

For a time-like interval, there exists some frame of reference where the two events occur at the same place, and the time between them from that point-of-view is 

$$
\tau=\frac{\sqrt{ct^2-x^2-y^2-z^2}}{c}
$$

  

For a spacelike interval, there exists some frame of reference where the the two events occur at the same time, and the distance between the two events from that point-of-view is 

$$
\chi=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2-ct^2}
$$



For a speed-of-light interval, there exist an infinity of frames of reference where the time and distance between the emission and absorption of the photon is negligible. If you accelerate along the vector from the source to the target, the time and Euclidean distance is reduced (by Bondi's k-factor). If you accelerate the other way, it is increased (by Bondi's k-factor)

# Unfamiliarity or Disdain

Does everything above seem reasonable?

The question is whether you are not familiar with the idea, or if you are familiar, but you view the idea with disdain. If you're already familiar with the description that I just gave, then it may be a psychological or philosophical problem... not because it is unfamiliar, but because it seems insane.

For instance, Voyager 1 is now 172 Astronomical Units from the Sun. And so if I asked "How far has Voyager 1 traveled since it launched from Earth." you would probably be inclined to say "around 172 AU'. That's not an unreasonable answer, but what if I said, "Properly, it has not gone anywhere, but rather, the sun and earth have moved away from it"? If you *can* entertain that idea, that the actual "proper" distance traveled by Voyager 1 is no distance at all, then you are equipped to psychologically and philosophically understand the Minkowski metric. If, on the other hand, you view the idea with disdain, or as insane, it is possible that you are psychologically incompatible with the Minkowski metric, as a measure of "proper" time or "proper" distance.
