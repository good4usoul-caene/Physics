"Objective" and "Subjective" are both [[Know your Homonyms|Homonyms]].  Confusing the definitions can result in rhetorical flourishes that end in unnecessary claims of [[Never say Meaningless|meaninglessness]].

**Example:**

 ==🟠JL(3305.10): "As the [[Abbreviations|ETETE]] proves if the [[Abbreviations|SOL]] is invariant, although simultaneity can be consistently defined within one [[Abbreviations|IRF]], it loses its "objective meaning" because it becomes observer dependent."==

The conclusion appears compelling until we ask:
> Which definition of *objective* is being used?

- **Objective**
	- (Definition 1) not influenced by personal feelings or opinions in considering and representing facts. Contrasted with [subjective](https://www.bing.com/search?q=define+subjective&FORM=DCTRQY "https://www.bing.com/search?q=define+subjective&FORM=DCTRQY").  
		- "an objective judgment"
	- (2) not dependent on the mind for existence; actual:  
		- "An objective reality"

# Observer Dependency is Objective

Observer dependency satisfies both definitions of objective.  Consider the following examples:

| Perspective 1                                   | Perspective 2                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| A person steps on an ant                        | An ant gets stepped on                                  |
| One person sees events A and B are simultaneous | Another person sees events A and B are not simultaneous |
Acknowledging that a fact depends on perspective does not make that fact subjective.  

The perspectives themselves may differ, but the relationships within each perspective remain factual.

A velocity-dependent simultaneity relation is still objectively determined once the velocity has been specified.

# Simultaneity is not an opinion

Simultaneity is not influenced by personal feelings or opinions. Is simultaneity dependent on mind?   [[Distant Simultaneity]] is fully determined by the instantaneous velocity of the chosen [[Point-of-View]].   A mind can *choose* which velocity to consider, but once that choice is made, the simultaneity of relation follows as a matter of fact.  
No additional opinions are required
  
 - Subjective 
	 - (1) Influenced by personal feelings, tastes, or opinions. 
		 - "his views are highly subjective" · 
	 - (2) dependent on the mind or on an individual's perception for its existence.  

Neither definition describes simultaneity.

Simultaneity does not change because someone changes their opinion.

Nor does simultaneity cease to exist when nobody observes it.

# Perspective is not Perception

A common confusion occurs when _perspective_ is conflated with _perception_.

A reference frame is a perspective.

An opinion is a perception.

These are not the same thing.

Simultaneity requires a perspective, but it does not require actual perception.

It is determined by the geometry of spacetime and the chosen state of motion, not by the beliefs of an observer.

Simultaneity is not based or influenced on personal feelings, tastes, or opinions. Except that we can imagine different points-of-view, simultaneity is not ifluenced by mind. 

Simultaneity is well and fully defined by a velocity of a single point-of-view.  It requires *perspective*, but not actual *perception*.

## Conclusion

The statement

> "Simultaneity loses its objective meaning because it is observer dependent"

works rhetorically because it quietly shifts between different meanings of _objective_.

Observer-dependent does **not** imply opinion-dependent.

Frame-dependent does **not** imply mind-dependent.

Before declaring a concept "subjective," "non-objective," or "[[Never say Meaningless|meaningless]]," it is worth [[Pin Yourself Down|pinning down]] exactly which definition is being used.
