>By "impose" and "calibrate" here, I do absolutely mean "physically modify the clocks or rulers". For our particular situation we are imagining a specific observer at the center of a rotating rim, who demands that passing conveyor belts have their rulers stretched, and their clocks sped up, and/or desynced to match his expectations. The rulers and clocks are assumed to already be in place, and either they do or do not meet with the expectations of the axle observer. To "calibrate the clocks and rulers by stretching or speeding up" avoids the ambiguity of "assigning coordinates to clocks and rulers".
>
>In this discussion, calibration retains its ordinary meaning of correcting an instrument. The issue is that two different observers employ different standards of correctness. A clock that is corrected according to one observer's synchronization convention will generally be miscalibrated according to the other observer's convention.
>
For ruler scale, clock rate, and synchronization, there exist two competing standards of correction, one associated with the conveyor frame and one associated with the axle frame. Correcting the instruments according to one standard generally prevents them from being simultaneously corrected according to the other.

# Requirement for Distant Simultaneity to be Well-Defined
 - A single world-line.  
 - An event on that world-line

# Requirement for Distant Simultaneity to be Imposed

Imposing a synchronization onto distant clocks is conventional and difficult to do.  See [[Einstein Synchronization Convention]] 

Given a single observation at any instant from any [[Point-of-View]], [[Distant Simultaneity]] is perfectly well defined globally.  

However, one does not, instantaneously create a universe full of physical [[Einstein Synchronization Convention|Synchronized Clocks]] in that instant.  [[Ghost Clock]]s, yes, but physical clocks, no.

Simultaneity is established globally, though not imposed globally from a single point of view at a single instant.

Imposing a synchronization is different from acknowledging simultaneity. 

Imposing synchronization can be done in a small region. even with clocks that are not comovig with the synchronizing signal.

  Non-inertial signal sources don't do a very good job of Imposing synchronization over large regions.  

  ECI imposes sync on the scale of earth and its satellites.  Earth's trajectory is fairly straight on the scale of hours or days.
