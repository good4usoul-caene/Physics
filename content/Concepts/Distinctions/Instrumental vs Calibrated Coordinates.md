
# Is Time Merely What a Clock Measures?


>[!info]- This page inspired by 3318.6
>[Is there a solid counter-argument against Dingle's old objection to Relativity Theory? | ResearchGate (3318.6), AA](https://www.researchgate.net/post/Is_there_a_solid_counter-argument_against_Dingles_old_objection_to_Relativity_Theory/3318)

One philosophy of special and general relativity holds that time is simply what clocks measure, and distance is simply what rulers measure. This viewpoint naturally leads to generalized coordinate systems in which clocks, rulers, and synchronization conventions define the coordinates themselves.

Under this view, asking whether clocks are "really synchronized" or "really running at the correct rate" begins to sound meaningless. If a ruler reads one meter, then that is one meter by definition. If clocks A and B both read 2:00, then they are synchronized by definition.

My own framework begins from a different premise. Clocks and rulers are instruments that attempt to represent an underlying coordinate system. Consequently, clocks, rulers, and synchronization conventions may be calibrated correctly or incorrectly relative to that system.

| Instrumental View                                        | Calibration View                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Time is what clocks measure                              | Clocks may measure time correctly or incorrectly.                                                                |
| Distance is what rulers measure                          | Rulers may measure distance correctly or incorrectly.                                                            |
| Simultaneity is determined by synchronization convention | Synchronization conventions may be judged correct or incorrect.                                                  |
| No independent standard exists beyond the instruments.   | Instruments are evaluated against an independent standard.                                                       |
| Transformation Equations may change the physics          | Transformation Equations may impose correct or incorrect calibration to physical or [[ghost clocks]] and rulers. |

My framework is that clocks and rulers are instruments that attempt to represent a coordinate system, and that such instruments,
1.  **when under acceleration**, are "slipping" and thus, inevitably unreliable, (No carpenter will trust a measure when he knows his ruler has slipped) 
2. **when inertial**, can only be made reliable if they are *correctly* synchronized, and *correctly* calibrated.

In contrast to the instrumental view, I assume that:
- There is a unique correct rate for an inertial clock.  
- There is a unique correct length for an inertial ruler.
- There is a unique correct synchronization for sets of inertial comoving clocks.

When the distances, rates, or synchronization is incorrectly set, of course, they arrive at incorrect measures for velocities, such as the speed of light.
