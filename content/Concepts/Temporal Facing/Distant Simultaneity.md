As for whether simultaneity describes the real world, it's much like asking whether "in front of you" describes the real world. 

"In front of you" is not the same as "in front of me", but both describe the real world. 

It is exactly the same with simultaneity, except for one major issue: 

In the following sense, simultaneity MAY not describe the real world--and that is in the exact same sense that the future may not describe the real world. Only past events $$ct < -|x|$$ are unchangeable reality. Simultaneous events are among those that may be changeable, but not by the observer. $$ct>|x|$$ are events in the future that we might influence.  - Only past events are unchangeable reality; 
 - Simultaneity and “the future” are ways we talk about the world, not structures the world is obliged to honor invariantly. 
 - But even so, the coordinates of these will-they-won't-they-happen events is perfectly well defined, once the observer's "[[here and now]]" is defined.. 
 - It is just whether they will happen or not that we cannot know (immediately).
