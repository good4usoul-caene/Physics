

### Lagrangian:

$$L=KE(\dot x)-V(x)$$
where
$$\dot x \equiv \frac{dx}{dt}
$$
$V$ is potential energy, and hence only a function of the particle's position.  $KE$ is kinetic energy, and hence only a function of the particle's veloccity

## The  Euler Lagrange Equation

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=0$$

The partial derivatives of the whole Lagrangian become total derivatives of the parts of the Lagrangian.
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{d(KE(\dot x))}{d\dot x}\right)+\frac{d(V(x))}{dx}=0$$
Using some physical identities, this becomes
$$\frac{d}{dt}\vec p-F=0$$
$$\vec F=-\frac{d\vec p}{dt}$$
This is the Impulse Momentum Law
