A ghost clock may be a [[Euclidean Points and Parallel World Lines|Euclidean Point]] or an accelerating point in space that has some time-value associated with it.

A ghost clock might be calibrated to measure a speed of time that a real clock in that space might not measure.  

A ghost clock can potentially move faster than the speed of light in for instance, the [[Head Turning Coordinates]] as you turn your head with radial velocity $\omega$, the velocity of a point straight in front of your eyes at a distance $r$ travels at a speed of $v=r\omega$.  

There is literally nothing preventing you from staring at a point in space $(r)$ billions of light years in front of you, and turning your head, imagining that point swinging through the sky.  
If $$r\omega>c$$ then the point is traveling faster than the speed of light.  It is not impossible to define a point traveling this way, but it *is* impossible for any physical body to actually occupy the location of this point.  No physical objects can travel at speeds greater than the speed of light.

You can also associate the time on your own watch with distant points in space, even though your own watch has no influence on what clocks at those distant points actually may read.

That being said, so long as the velocity of the points actually ARE under the speed of light, it is possible to [calibrate] them so that the person turning his head actually sees the clocks as though they are synchronized, as visualized here:  [Center-Synchronization](https://www.desmos.com/calculator/uqsbrtz3mw).
