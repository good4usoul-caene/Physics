Numbers really do not have any meaning unless **units** are put after them.  

That's not to say they cannot be approached without assigning meaning.  I have seen students happily multiply $3^{10} \times 2^{-14}$  and arrive at an answer if $6\times 10^4$ or $6\times 10^{24}$ and there might be teachers who might give partial credit for such an answer, even though it is 8 or 28 orders of magnitude too large.  Such errors are almost inevitable when numbers are treated completely in the abstract.  When **units** are imagined, the problem here might be interpreted as an area of a rectangle.  One would notice that $3^{10}$ is a very big length while $2^{-14}$ is an even smaller length, and so the area obtained ought to be somewhere between big and small, with the small being favored.

Moreover, the calculator does not operate on knowing the units of the numbers.  It actually does treat the numbers entirely abstractly.  It explicitly operates on pure adjectives.   This is much in the way one can know what color is obtained by combining red and green even though no object is painted this color.  Or one might combine the adjectives of thin and muscular into "wiry" without assigning the adjectives to any particular person.
  
Confirm for yourself:  Does 5 have any meaning at all before you imagine 5 dots, 5 arbitrary forms, 5 units of length?  In some sense it does, just as "red" has meaning, even if it is not assigned to an object.

However, if you think of the color red, you probably imagine some red surface.  Without that surface, (the noun), you can't think of the color red, except for the sound of the word.  

But let's say you have some capacity wherein you can imagine the number 5 and the color red in an entirely abstract way.  If I had never interacted with a [[Large Language Model]] I would probably say I didn't believe you.  However, knowing that such Artificial Intelligence can wax quite eloquently about the number 5 and the color red just by manipulation of language itself, I do understand that there could be minds out there working completely off of symbol memorization and have no internal picture of 5 or red that requires units.

That being said, physics is a regime where all numbers -- even unitless ones -- have distinguishable operational descriptions.  One does not obtain an angle, for instance, without imagining a radius and an arclength, or a ratio between the swept out area and the full circle.  

So pedagogically speaking, it is always beneficial to think about what units are implied, even when they cancel out; see [[Unitlessness]]
