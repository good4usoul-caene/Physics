Invariant and Relative Quantities

Invariant and Relative Quantities

Oct 05, 20262 min read

Velocity is a “relative” or “perspectival” quantity while events, themselves, such as a clock ticking a certain quantity, is called an invariant quantity. Relative quantities are quantities that can be changed by changing your perspective, without touching or communicating an object. Invariant quantities are things you can’t change without touching or influencing the object having the measured property. In general the existence of an event cannot be changed, but it’s location in time and space is

Can You Make it Go Away?

You can tell if something is relative or absolute by considering whether you can “make it go away”. The clock having a reading of is something you could ignore, but if it has that time, your ignorance of it doesn’t influence the truth of the reading.

However, if the clock is passing by at you can make that velocity essentially “go away” simply by walking along with it at the same speed.

If you have the means to boost yourself to arbitrary velocities, you can, without touching the clock, make it so that from your own perspective, the clock may have any velocity you want (up to the speed of light.)

”Without Touching It”

Some might object, saying “But I could take out the clock’s batteries, or stop its pendulum so that it doesn’t read 3 PM. So can you make the reading go away? Yes, but once you touch the clock, you’re creating other events that no one else can make “go away” unless they can physically pre-empt or undo your act.
